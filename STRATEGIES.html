Level 6 strategies, interactive

How ccc/contest.py writes short programs that submit a pattern k times. Everything below is simulated in the page by a port of the solver's board, ray, tick and submission logic; the programs were generated by the Python solver and re-checked here.

Page self-checks

    1 · Chords and useful positions

    Emitter d starts on corner d and only ever moves to the 2r−1 border cells around it (offset −(r−1)..r−1, the yellow-ringed slots). From each slot its beam covers one full chord of the board, and the 2r−1 chords tile the board once. At ±r it would sit on the next corner and shine along the border, so the solver never goes there. The opposite emitter at the negated offset (purple, dashed) covers the very same chord from the other end: chords(r) stores (k, o) → bitmask.

    2 · Hit counting

    All switched-on beams fire at once. A cell hit by exactly 2 beams becomes M (green), by 3 or more is destroyed back to O (red); 0 or 1 hit leaves it alone. count_hits gets both answers for the whole board at once: every cell is a bit, every beam a bitmask, and three integers form a saturating per-cell counter.

    The one / two / many adder 
    for m in beams:            # m: bitmask of a beam
    many |= two & m        # 2 hits + 1 = 3+
    carry = one & m
    one  ^= m              # low bit of the count
    two = (two ^ carry) & ~many
return two & ~many, many   # exactly 2, 3+
    Bit i = cell i (row-major, the numbers on the board). Each line updates all 19 counters in parallel.
    
    
    
  
    
  
    

    


    
  
    3 · Pattern, halo and submission
    
  
    After every tick the pattern is submitted when all pattern cells are M (blue outline) and no halo cell is M (dashed: interior cells touching the pattern). Then only the pattern cells clear to O; matter elsewhere stays.
  Since the halo must be clean, after each submission the zone that matters is empty again, so every repetition is the same problem. Click cells to add or remove matter.
    
  
    
    
    
  
    
  
  
    

    


    
  
    4 · Strategy players
    
  
    Each program comes from ccc.contest and was validated with Python valid6; the badge re-runs it here.
  baseline: a driver pair walks (BFS) to cross on one unbuilt pattern cell at a time, four greedy helpers add crossings, and it rebuilds from scratch for every submission.
  Cycles fire a short list of fixed emitter configurations again and again: a lead-in walk (setup), then A → B → …, with a submission after every full round.
  frame is a 1-config cycle, pair two configs (A may leave stray halo matter that B's 3+ hits wipe), triple/cover three, toggle parks emitters once and only switches them on and off.

    5 · Why repeats are cheap
    
  
    The baseline rebuilds the pattern from scratch each time, so its length grows by a whole build per submission. A cycle pays its setup once and then m · gap ticks per submission:
  setup + 1 + (m·k − 1)·gap. Real line counts from baseline6 and pair6 for k = 1..10 on each example pattern.
